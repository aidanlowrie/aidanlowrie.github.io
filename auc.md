At AUC, my work sat at the boundary between biological mechanism, behaviour, and
ethics. That combination mattered. It trained me to move between what is
happening in a system and what that means in practice.

Brain and Cognition was the clearest example: neural communication, emotion,
mental-health aetiology, and neurobiology of behaviour were taught with explicit
attention to interpretation and social implications. Molecular Cell Biology ran
in parallel at a different scale: replication and repair, membranes and
transport, intracellular organisation, signalling, and cell-cycle control.
Immunology then added a full system-level layer, from innate/adaptive structure
to immune failure, vaccination, and immunotherapy contexts.

Bioethics completed the picture. Rather than treating ethics as commentary after
the science, the course structure made it part of core analytical work: care,
reproduction, end-of-life decisions, and the implications of genetic knowledge.

The result was a style of thinking I still use: understand mechanism precisely,
then test whether the interpretation is actually justified, then ask what
follows for real decisions.
