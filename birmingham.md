This page summarises what I focused on during my time at Birmingham, based on my
year-by-year course notes.

## Year One: Molecular foundations and core chemical biology

Year one was where I built the core life-science and chemistry toolkit:
genetics, metabolism, and organic chemistry, with additional training in
science communication and policy.

### Genetics (Year One)

Main focus areas:

- Genomes and genome organisation.
- DNA replication: origins, replication forks, replisome, proofreading, and
  eukaryotic replication.
- Transcription and translation: prokaryotic and eukaryotic transcription,
  genetic code, tRNA/ribosome function, and translation mechanics.
- Mitosis and meiosis: mechanics, crossing over, and chromosomal variation.
- DNA damage and mutation: classes of mutation, replication error, DNA damage,
  and repair pathways.
- Inheritance and variation: Mendelian inheritance and pedigree reasoning.
- Early bacterial genetics ideas: transformation, transduction, and conjugation.

### Organic Chemistry (Year One)

Main focus areas:

- Alkenes and alkynes: oxidative/reductive chemistry, Diels-Alder, and alkyne
  reactivity.
- Carbonyl chemistry: nomenclature, MO perspective, reactivity, keto-enol
  tautomerism, oxidation routes.
- Aldehyde/ketone reactions: Wittig chemistry, nucleophilic addition, reversible
  vs irreversible pathways, imines and enamines.
- Carboxylic acid derivatives: nucleophilic addition-elimination, relative
  reactivity, ester/amide formation and hydrolysis.
- Conjugation, acetals, aromaticity, electrophilic aromatic substitution, and
  aromatic synthesis strategy.

### Metabolism (Year One)

Main focus areas:

- Enzyme nomenclature and metabolic pathway logic.
- Glycolysis, TCA cycle, and gluconeogenesis, including regulation.
- Allostery and haemoglobin oxygen-binding behaviour.
- Carbohydrate metabolism and metabolic integration.
- Lipid metabolism: lipid function, storage, oxidation, and synthesis.
- Applied metabolic reasoning via case-study work (including obesity and
  clinical-style metabolism problems).
- Early metabolomics exposure in the context of global metabolism.

### Cross-disciplinary modules (Year One)

- Art of Persuasion: scientific communication and argumentation.
- From Research to Policy: translation from scientific evidence to policy.

## Year Two: Regulation, cell systems, and experimental method

Year two shifted from foundations to system-level biology and more explicit
methodological thinking.

### Semester One

#### Cell Biology

Main focus areas:

- Membrane structure and membrane transport.
- Cytoskeleton and gene expression links.
- Daphnia pre-lab and experiment-oriented biological reasoning.

#### Inorganic Chemistry 2A

Main focus areas:

- Molecular symmetry, point groups, and character tables.
- Matrix/representation methods and group-theory applications.
- Molecular vibrations.
- Transition metal complex bonding and electronic spectra.
- Associated laboratory skills and practicals.

#### Science and Nature

- Broader scientific framing module running alongside technical content.

### Semester Two

#### Cell Biology

Main focus areas:

- Cell cycle and apoptosis.
- Embryology and developmental cell biology.
- Stem cells (embryonic, induced pluripotent, and adult stem-cell systems).
- Microscopy.
- Cell signalling and plant structure/function.

#### Genetics

Main focus areas:

- Bacterial gene regulation (operons, control architecture, global regulation).
- In vivo and in vitro regulatory methods.
- Eukaryotic gene expression control in chromatin context.
- Pre-initiation complex assembly and activation in chromatin.
- Chromosome structure, segregation, and bacterial genome dynamics.
- Practical promoter-probe style experimental work.

#### Inorganic Chemistry

Main focus areas:

- Solid-state synthesis.
- Crystal structure and X-ray diffraction.
- Solid and non-stoichiometric defects.
- Organometallic stability, electron counting, and synergic bonding models.

#### Interdisciplinarity

- Structured reflection and seminar-driven interdisciplinary integration.

## Final Year: Neurobiology, motivation, and omics/data integration

Final year was the most direct bridge into my later research interests:
mechanism, cognition, and data-rich biological measurement.

### Cellular Neurobiology

Main focus areas:

- Cellular mechanisms of learning and memory.
- LTP evidence in mammalian associative learning.
- Circuit tools: optogenetics and thermogenetics.
- Comparative cognition and neurobiology across taxa (primates, birds, insects,
  cephalopods).
- Axon guidance, circuit connectivity, and topographic mapping.
- Neurotrophins, structural plasticity, and glial roles in brain function.
- Drosophila as a circuit model system.

### What Motivates You

Main focus areas:

- Psychobiological basis of motivation.
- Disorders of motivation, with dopamine as a central explanatory thread.
- Coursework-led synthesis in essay form (module assessed via coursework).

### Omics

Main focus areas:

- Systems-level biological measurement across genomics/transcriptomics/
  proteomics/metabolomics framing.
- Critical comparison of omics technologies and experiment design choices.
- Proteomics workflows: sample prep, LC-MS, shotgun and gel-based strategies,
  peptide identification logic, and interpretation.
- Metabolomics applications and hypothesis generation from high-dimensional data
  (including environmental toxicology case material).
- Data analysis, visualisation, and interpretation as core parts of biological
  inference.

### Data Science and Practical Project

- Project-oriented and analytical workstreams that complemented module study and
  reinforced applied quantitative thinking.

## Through-line across Birmingham

Across the full period, the strongest through-lines were:

- Moving from molecular mechanism to system-level explanation.
- Treating regulation and signalling as the central logic of biological
  behaviour.
- Building comfort with experimental design, data interpretation, and
  method selection.
- Increasingly linking biology to computation and quantitative modelling.
