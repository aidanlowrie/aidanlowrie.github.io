This page summarises what I focused on during my Birmingham years from my
year-by-year course notes.

## Year One: Molecular and chemical foundations

The first year was about building a base in genetics, metabolism, and organic
chemistry, while also developing communication and policy literacy.

| Module | What I focused on | Methods and skills I built | Assessment pattern in notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Genetics (LC Genetics I) | Genomes; DNA replication; transcription/translation; mitosis/meiosis; mutation and DNA repair; Mendelian inheritance; early bacterial genetics concepts (transformation/transduction/conjugation). | Mechanistic pathway reasoning, genotype-to-phenotype logic, mutation classification, pedigree interpretation. | Weekly note structure; specific weighting not captured in module index notes. |
| Organic Chemistry B | Alkenes/alkynes; carbonyl chemistry; aldehyde/ketone reactions; carboxylic acid derivatives; aromaticity and EAS; synthesis of complex aromatic molecules. | Reaction mechanism analysis, electrophile/nucleophile reasoning, synthesis planning. | Weekly note structure; specific weighting not captured in module index notes. |
| Metabolism | Enzymes, glycolysis, TCA, gluconeogenesis, allostery and haemoglobin, carbohydrate and lipid metabolism, systems-level metabolism. | Pathway integration, physiological interpretation, biochemical case-study reasoning. | Includes case-study work (for example obesity case study; one case noted as 50% of module grade). |
| Art of Persuasion B | Scientific persuasion and argument quality. | Structured argument, communication for non-specialist audiences. | Not detailed in extracted notes. |
| From Research to Policy II | Translating evidence into policy framing. | Policy-oriented interpretation of research evidence. | Not detailed in extracted notes. |

## How focus changed across the Birmingham years

- **Year One:** build hard molecular and chemical fundamentals.
- **Year Two:** move into regulation, chromatin, cell systems, and experimental
  contexts.
- **Final Year:** integrate mechanism with behaviour/cognition and scale up into
  high-dimensional omics thinking.
- **Overall through-line:** a steady move from reductionist mechanism to systems
  interpretation, then toward computational and data-led biological inference.
